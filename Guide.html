Insights for students

Simple habits that match what OmniBand measures.

Posture

Keep your screen at eye level, feet flat, back supported and wrists neutral. OmniBand estimates posture from wrist tilt only, so treat it as a nudge, not a measurement.

Breaks and movement

Stand up every 40 minutes for 3 to 5 minutes. Look at something 20 feet away for 20 seconds every 20 minutes. Short walks improve focus.

Stress

Try slow breathing (4 seconds in, 6 seconds out) for 2 minutes, drink water and sleep 7 to 8 hours. If stress stays high for days, talk to someone you trust or your college counsellor.

Suspected heart attack: act now

Warning signs: chest pressure, tightness or pain lasting more than a few minutes, spreading to the arm, jaw, neck or back; cold sweat, breathlessness, nausea or light-headedness. Symptoms can be milder in women and people with diabetes.

  1. Call 112 (or ambulance 108) immediately. Do not wait to see if it passes, and do not drive yourself.
  2. Sit down, stay calm and loosen tight clothing. Ask someone to meet the ambulance.
  3. Chew one regular aspirin tablet (300 to 325 mg, or about four low-dose 75 to 81 mg tablets), unless you are allergic, have active bleeding, or a doctor told you not to take it.
  4. If a doctor has prescribed you a nitroglycerin (GTN) tablet, place it under the tongue as instructed. Never use someone else's.
  5. If the person collapses and is not breathing normally, start CPR (push hard and fast in the centre of the chest) and use an AED if one is nearby.

The three medicines used in a heart attack

MedicineWho can use itWhat it does
AspirinAnyone who is not allergic. First aid.Slows clot growth in the blocked artery. Chew it, do not swallow whole.
Nitroglycerin (GTN), under the tongueOnly if prescribed to that person.Widens blood vessels and eases chest pain. Avoid with very low blood pressure or recent sildenafil or tadalafil use.
Antiplatelet and clot-dissolving drugs (such as clopidogrel or ticagrelor, with heparin)Hospital team only.Keep the artery open until it can be reopened. Do not self-medicate with these.

Antacids do not treat chest pain. If you are unsure whether it is the heart, call emergency services.
